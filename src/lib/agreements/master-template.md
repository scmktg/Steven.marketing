# MARKETING SERVICES & PERFORMANCE COMMISSION AGREEMENT
## DRAFT FOR INDEPENDENT AUSTRALIAN LEGAL REVIEW — NOT APPROVED FOR SIGNATURE

This Marketing Services and Performance Commission Agreement (Agreement) is made between the Agency and Client described in Schedule A. The Schedules, including any expressly negotiated special conditions, form part of this Agreement. If there is inconsistency, the signed Special Conditions in Schedule A prevail, followed by Schedule B and then the general clauses.

## 1. Parties and appointment
1.1 The Agency is [[AGENCY_LEGAL_NAME]], ABN [[AGENCY_ABN]], trading as Steven Marketing, at [[AGENCY_BUSINESS_ADDRESS]] (Agency).
1.2 The Client is [[CLIENT_LEGAL_NAME]], ABN/ACN [[CLIENT_ABN_ACN]], trading as [[CLIENT_DISPLAY_NAME]], at [[CLIENT_ADDRESS]] (Client).
1.3 The Client appoints the Agency to provide the services within the agreed Scope in Schedule A. The parties are independent contractors; nothing creates employment, partnership, a franchise or authority for one party to bind the other.
1.4 Each signatory warrants they are authorised to sign for their organisation.

## 2. Services and approval
2.1 The Agency will perform reasonable professional marketing services within the Scope. These may include customer and competitor research, campaign strategy, paid advertising, SEO, landing pages, conversion optimisation, analytics, tracking, content and reporting, as expressly selected in Schedule A.
2.2 The Agency will use reasonable skill and care but does not promise a specific revenue, ranking, conversion or advertising result. Platform decisions, competition and customer behaviour are outside the Agency's control.
2.3 Material scope changes, new channels, budgets or third-party costs require recorded approval. Neither party may change signed financial terms unilaterally.
2.4 The Agency will provide periodic written reporting and maintain reasonable records of material campaign work, assets and tracking changes. The Client must review requests for approval promptly.

## 3. Client cooperation, accounts and compliance
3.1 The Client remains responsible for the accuracy and legality of product claims, pricing, fulfilment, warranties, regulated-product requirements and its customer communications.
3.2 The Client will provide lawful, timely access to relevant accounts and accurate sales, refund, advertising and analytics reporting, and will designate an authorised contact. The Agency will request only the permissions reasonably required for the Scope.
3.3 Advertising, merchant, analytics, domain, website, business email and payment accounts created for the Client should, wherever practical, be registered in the Client's name and remain under the Client's ultimate control. The Agency may receive delegated access.
3.4 Each party will comply with applicable Australian marketing, privacy and consumer laws and relevant platform policies within its responsibilities. Personal data may be used only for the agreed services and according to applicable law and an agreed data-processing schedule, if required.
3.5 The Client may appoint other advisers. It must not knowingly obstruct agreed reporting or misrepresent results; the Agency must not impede reasonable access to Client-owned accounts.

## 4. Contract term
4.1 The Agreement starts on the Commencement Date in Schedule A and continues for the Initial Commitment Period stated there (proposed default: three months), unless ended sooner under clause 12.2.
4.2 After the Initial Commitment Period, the Agreement continues on a rolling monthly basis unless either party gives the Ordinary Notice Period in Schedule A (proposed default: 30 calendar days) in writing. To keep reporting and the Baseline calculation consistent, ordinary termination takes effect at the end of the first full Reporting Period that ends at least the Ordinary Notice Period after notice is given, and never before the Initial Commitment Period ends. Earlier termination for cause under clause 12.2 remains available.
4.3 Each commission Reporting Period is a monthly anniversary period beginning on the numerical day of the Commencement Date and ending the day before the next anniversary. Where a month does not contain that day, use its final calendar day; each following anniversary is calculated from the original Commencement Date, not the previously adjusted date.
4.4 There is no automatic new fixed term. Changes to the duration require both parties' written acceptance.

## 5. Performance-based remuneration
5.1 The applicable fee model, Eligible Revenue definition, fixed Baseline, marginal commission bands, any fixed fees, reporting source and GST treatment are stated in Schedule B. The Agency earns commission only where the agreed fee formula generates a positive amount. Other clients may select a different fee model in Schedule B.
5.2 Unless Schedule B expressly states otherwise, Growth for a Reporting Period equals the greater of zero and (Eligible Revenue for that period minus the fixed Baseline). The Baseline is the negotiated monthly reference amount, not a target that the Agency guarantees.
5.3 Where a tiered model applies, each percentage applies only to the portion of Growth inside its band, not to the entire amount retrospectively. Example only: the first $20,000 of Growth at 12%, the next $30,000 at 10%, and amounts above $50,000 at 8%.
5.4 Eligible Revenue must be defined consistently with the Baseline: specify product/booking scope, source system, sales taxes or GST, shipping, discounts, refunds, cancellations, reversals, chargebacks, rebates, related-party sales, other channels and currency conversion in Schedule B. The definition must not be changed retrospectively except by signed variation or correction of verified reporting errors.
5.5 Refunds and reversals discovered after period closure will be adjusted in the next commission statement, with an itemised explanation; no amount is counted twice. The parties will agree a reasonable treatment for atypical bulk orders or structural business changes rather than permitting one party to unilaterally rebase or exclude revenue.
5.6 The Agency will give the Client a period statement explaining sources, dates, eligible revenue, Baseline, growth, each tier and the commission. Incomplete daily information produces a provisional estimate only and is not invoiced as final.
5.7 The Client may query a statement within 14 days of receipt, stating the disputed item and evidence. Undisputed amounts remain payable. Neither party may knowingly conceal records or manipulate the selected reporting source.

## 6. Invoicing, GST and third-party costs
6.1 Unless Schedule A states otherwise, an invoice is issued after the end of the Reporting Period, following reconciliation, and is due 14 calendar days after issue. All invoiced amounts are in Australian dollars.
6.2 Fees are exclusive of GST unless Schedule A expressly says they include GST. If GST is payable, it will be added to a compliant tax invoice. Nothing in this Agreement asserts that the Agency is GST-registered.
6.3 The Client separately funds its own approved advertising and platform expenses, preferably paying platforms directly. Agency commissions are not deducted from the Client's advertising budget unless expressly agreed.
6.4 Reasonable, documented and pre-approved out-of-pocket costs may be invoiced at cost. The Agency may not incur additional material costs without approval.
6.5 A genuine, good-faith invoice dispute is not grounds to suspend otherwise paid-for Client-owned assets. For an undisputed overdue amount, the Agency may suspend future services on seven days' written warning, acting reasonably and without blocking the Client's own business accounts.

## 7. Reporting, verification and audit
7.1 The Client will provide the agreed read-only sales data needed to verify both active-period and valid post-termination commissions. Dashboard figures are provisional until the applicable statement is reconciled.
7.2 Each party may request supporting calculations and source reports reasonably necessary to verify a commission, subject to privacy, confidentiality and security. Neither party is required to disclose unrelated customer personal information.
7.3 Within 30 days of a reasonably supported question about an invoice, the parties will reconcile material discrepancies in good faith. The Agency may inspect records limited to the agreed metrics, on reasonable notice, no more than twice per year unless material error is identified, through a secure method.
7.4 Records and a final account of accrued commissions will be kept for the legally required period or at least the period reasonably necessary to resolve outstanding contractual payment obligations.

## 8. Intellectual property and marketing infrastructure
8.1 The Client owns its pre-existing trademarks, website, domains, customer data, advertising accounts and other Client Materials.
8.2 The Agency owns its pre-existing methods, reusable templates, code libraries, know-how, analytical frameworks and generic tools (Agency Background Materials). The Agency grants the Client a perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive licence to the Agency Background Materials to the extent incorporated in paid-for deliverables and needed to continue using those deliverables for its business, excluding separate licensed third-party products.
8.3 Subject to payment of undisputed amounts due for the relevant work, the Agency assigns to the Client the intellectual property it owns in specifically commissioned deliverables created solely for the Client under the Scope, excluding Agency Background Materials and third-party materials. Each party will execute reasonable further documents to give this effect.
8.4 Third-party assets, fonts, software, stock media and advertising platforms remain subject to their own licences. The Agency will identify material licence restrictions before delivery.
8.5 The Client may keep operating the sites, campaigns, content and integrations paid for after termination, subject to applicable licences and the specifically negotiated, finite trailing commission in clause 13. There is no requirement to delete Client-owned marketing systems or to retain the Agency as operator.
8.6 The Agency may describe the Client publicly as a customer or publish a case study only with the Client's written permission.

## 9. Confidentiality and data protection
9.1 Each party must protect the other's non-public commercial, technical, pricing, customer and financial information using reasonable safeguards; use it only to perform or enforce this Agreement; disclose it only to personnel or advisers bound by appropriate obligations; and notify the other promptly of a material compromise relevant to the engagement.
9.2 Confidentiality does not apply to information lawfully public, independently developed without use of confidential information, already lawfully known, or required to be disclosed by law (subject to lawful prior notice where practical).
9.3 Each party must return or securely delete the other's confidential materials on reasonable request after termination, except information retained by law, standard secure backups or reasonably necessary records for resolving accrued obligations.
9.4 Client data and marketing-system access is not itself a grant of permission to contact customers or conduct direct marketing independently of the Client's lawful authority.

## 10. Warranties, risk and liability
10.1 Each party warrants it has authority to perform its obligations and will use reasonable care. Neither party guarantees uninterrupted third-party systems.
10.2 To the extent permitted by law, neither party is liable to the other for remote or consequential loss, including speculative anticipated profits, except where exclusion is prohibited by law.
10.3 Subject to clause 10.4, each party's aggregate liability for claims under this Agreement is capped at the greater of (a) fees paid or properly payable to the Agency over the preceding six complete Reporting Periods and (b) the fixed minimum liability amount negotiated in Schedule A. This does not reduce the Client's obligation to pay validly earned fees.
10.4 The cap does not apply to fraud, deliberate misconduct, payment obligations, infringement of the other party's intellectual property rights or liabilities that cannot lawfully be limited. Any additional uncapped confidentiality, privacy or security liability must be expressly negotiated in Schedule A.
10.5 Nothing excludes or restricts non-excludable rights under Australian law. The parties will take reasonable steps to mitigate avoidable loss.

## 11. Suspension and change controls
11.1 Where an external platform suspends an account, a security incident occurs or required Client approvals are withheld, the affected party will give prompt notice and cooperate on a reasonable workaround.
11.2 A material change to business ownership, product category, payment processing or reporting architecture will trigger a good-faith review of the measurement schedule. Until agreed in writing, the signed formula remains in force, except to the extent accurate measurement is impossible; in that case no unsupported commission is presumed due.
11.3 Approved changes must be documented in a dated variation accepted by both parties. Editing a dashboard draft does not vary an executed agreement.

## 12. Ending the agreement
12.1 Either party may terminate for convenience under clause 4, on written notice taking effect no earlier than the end of the Initial Commitment Period.
12.2 Either party may terminate immediately if the other commits a material breach and fails to remedy it within 14 calendar days after a written notice describing the breach and reasonable remedial steps, or if the breach cannot reasonably be remedied. Termination for insolvency is subject to applicable law.
12.3 On termination: (a) active services cease on the effective date; (b) the parties settle valid commissions already earned and approved third-party commitments; (c) the Agency provides a reasonable handover of Client-owned accounts, access and paid-for deliverables; and (d) access is revoked in an orderly and secure manner.
12.4 No arbitrary cancellation penalty applies. An agreed and properly disclosed setup or milestone fee for work already performed, and genuinely non-cancellable pre-approved third-party costs, may remain payable. Neither party may claim hypothetical future commissions for work that produces no actual qualifying revenue.
12.5 The Client may terminate without post-termination commission if it has validly terminated because of the Agency's unremedied material breach under clause 12.2. Commissions validly earned before termination are still due, subject to adjustment and any applicable legal rights.

## 13. Limited post-termination revenue protection — IMPORTANT COMMERCIAL TERM
13.1 The parties recognise that campaigns, SEO, copy, landing pages, tracking, customer acquisition and other documented work completed during the Term may generate measurable revenue after active services stop. This clause is a disclosed part of the performance-fee bargain, not an additional termination penalty.
13.2 If the Client terminates for convenience, or the Agency validly terminates for the Client's unremedied material breach, the Agency may earn a reduced commission on Qualifying Post-Termination Revenue only during the three successive monthly anniversary periods beginning on the day after the effective termination date (Tail Period). The Tail Period ends absolutely after the third period; there is no evergreen entitlement.
13.3 Qualifying Post-Termination Revenue means actual Eligible Revenue within the agreed product/channel scope that is reasonably connected to continuing Client use of, or measurable continuing benefit from, the Agency's Protected Work identified in Schedule C. Both parties will take account of contemporaneous reports, attribution information and later independent campaign activity. The Client will not be charged under this clause on clearly unrelated sales, new products/channels outside the agreed scope, or sales demonstrably attributable solely to independently implemented post-termination work. Both parties must reasonably cooperate to avoid double counting.
13.4 The calculation uses the signed Baseline, marginal tiers and eligible-revenue rules from Schedule B, applied to Qualifying Post-Termination Revenue for each Tail Period. The calculated amount is multiplied by: first period 100%, second period 75%, third period 50%. No fee is payable for a period without positive qualifying growth. Each period is reconciled after it ends, subject to reasonable refund adjustments.
13.5 Schedule A may specify a lower maximum amount payable over the entire Tail Period. If Schedule A is blank, the aggregate trailing commission is capped at three times the arithmetic average of the last three fully completed active-period commissions (or all available fully completed active periods if fewer than three). If there are no fully completed active periods, the cap will be expressly negotiated before signing; absent a stated cap, no trailing commission is payable. This ceiling is separate from commissions already earned before termination.
13.6 A dated Protected Work Register will identify significant work delivered during the engagement. Within ten business days after termination, the Agency may propose a final register using records made during the engagement. The Client may challenge any item reasonably; neither party may unilaterally enlarge the Scope or charge for previously unrelated work. Where the evidence cannot reasonably distinguish agency-influenced revenue, the disputed part will be resolved under clause 14 before invoicing.
13.7 The Client will provide only the limited, lawful sales reporting required to verify Tail Period commissions, either by secure read-only report or reasonable export; this does not entitle the Agency to ongoing administrator access to the Client's systems. The Agency will protect the information and delete it when no longer reasonably needed.
13.8 This clause does not restrict the Client from changing agencies, running its business, retaining paid-for assets, changing marketing strategy, or discontinuing Protected Work. There is no commission for a period in which no Protected Work continues to be used or to confer a reasonably evidenced benefit.
13.9 The parties acknowledge that enforceability depends on the circumstances, including small-business standard form protections. The Client has had this clause prominently disclosed and a genuine opportunity to negotiate the scope, percentage, duration and cap. Signatures alone do not make an otherwise unfair term enforceable.

## 14. Disputes
14.1 Before commencing proceedings, either party will provide written particulars and a reasonable opportunity for senior representatives to discuss the dispute in good faith within 14 days.
14.2 If unresolved within a further 14 days, the parties will consider non-binding mediation through an agreed independent mediator in [[GOVERNING_STATE_OR_TERRITORY]]. Either may seek urgent injunctive or protective relief where appropriate.
14.3 Undisputed sums remain due, and parties must continue reasonable protection of confidential information during a dispute.

## 15. General provisions
15.1 Notices may be sent to the contact email addresses in Schedule A and are taken received on acknowledgement or the next business day where no delivery failure is reported, except any formal notice that law requires by another method.
15.2 Neither party may assign this Agreement without the other's prior written consent, not unreasonably withheld, except to a genuine corporate successor that assumes the obligations and safeguards financial reporting.
15.3 This Agreement contains the parties' entire agreement on the Scope and fees, subject to mandatory law. Amendments require both parties' written agreement.
15.4 If a term is invalid, the remainder continues to the extent it can operate lawfully; invalid provisions are not automatically rewritten to the maximum commercially favourable scope.
15.5 The laws of [[GOVERNING_STATE_OR_TERRITORY]], Australia govern this Agreement. The courts of that jurisdiction have non-exclusive jurisdiction.
15.6 The parties consent to lawful electronic signatures and to delivery of electronic signed copies. Each authorised signatory intends to be legally bound. The signing service's completed certificate and unchanged signed PDF will be retained with the Client's account.
